Global standards, local rules

Professional pet care rests on standards — some set worldwide, some by your own country's licensing and trade bodies. Most of the record-keeping is standardised internationally, then national rules sit on top. FamilyCompass is built to mirror that.

What's the same almost everywhere

On the animal-health side, the World Small Animal Veterinary Association (WSAVA) sets the global backbone: its Vaccination Guidelines (the core vs. lifestyle split you'll see on a vaccination record) and its Global Nutrition Guidelines (weight, body condition and the thinking behind a kcal target) are used the same way worldwide. That's why a handover record means the same thing whether the owner is in Manchester, Munich, Miami or Mexico City.

On the business side, national trade and licensing bodies set the standard of excellence for boarding, day care, walking, sitting and grooming — and a clear, dated care record is exactly what those standards ask you to keep.

And what differs — the bodies and rules that set the professional standard in each region:

Region Pet-care & animal-health bodies What's standardised (global backbone) What's nationally specific
United States NAPPS (National Association of Professional Pet Sitters), IBPSA (boarding & day care), NDGAA (grooming); animal health via AVMA & AAHA WSAVA vaccination & nutrition backbone; dated care records Facility licensing varies by state; rabies vaccination legally required; boarding often requires proof of vaccination
United Kingdom Pet Industry Federation (PIF), NarpsUK (sitters & walkers), British Dog Groomers' Association Same WSAVA vaccination & nutrition backbone; body-condition scoring Animal Activities Licensing (2018) — boarding, day care & home boarding licensed by local councils; rabies-free, no routine rabies vaccine
Ireland Dept. of Agriculture, Food & the Marine, ISPCA; animal health via Veterinary Ireland WSAVA global guidelines; EU-harmonised welfare standards Dog Breeding Establishments & boarding registration rules; EU Pet Passport; rabies-free island regime
Germany & the EU BMEL (federal ministry), Deutscher Tierschutzbund; animal health via FECAVA WSAVA/FECAVA vaccination & nutrition guidelines § 11 Tierschutzgesetz permit to care for others' animals commercially; EU Pet Passport; mandatory rabies vaccination for travel
Mexico & Latin America SENASICA (Mexico), WSAVA regional associations, FIAVAC WSAVA core/lifestyle model & nutrition guidelines followed regionally Rabies endemic in parts of the region — rabies vaccination emphasised as core; licensing and welfare rules set at state/municipal level

FamilyCompass's UK / WSAVA / US region toggle is the practical expression of this: one global standard, with the regional differences the record actually needs. FamilyCompass is not affiliated with or endorsed by these organisations; the links are provided so you can read the standards for yourself.
