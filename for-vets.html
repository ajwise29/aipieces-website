FamilyCompass · For Veterinary Practices

Owners arrive at your consult with complete, dated records.

FamilyCompass helps pet owners keep an encrypted, dated record of medication, feeding, weight and symptoms between visits — so what reaches your consulting room is a history, not a recollection.

📈

Condition & weight record

A dated weight history and condition notes for each pet, alongside a breed-specific health watch list with weight range and lifespan information. The owner arrives able to show change over time, not describe it from memory.

💊

Medication & feeding log

Medications and prescriptions logged with dose and the exact time given, plus a feeding log with portions and a kcal target calculated from logged weight. A clear picture of what has actually been administered at home.

🔬

Litter & symptom monitoring

For cats, litter box monitoring records urine and stool events with faecal scoring; for any pet, symptoms are logged with dates. Owners bring counts and dates, giving your history-taking a head start.

What the owner brings to your consult

  • A dated medication and feeding log — not a recollection
  • Weight and condition history in one place
  • Litter, symptom and behaviour changes with dates attached
  • A one-tap summary written for the vet

🔒 Privacy

Global standards, local rules

The record an owner keeps is only useful to you if it speaks your language. Most of pet health record-keeping is standardised worldwide — then a handful of national rules sit on top. FamilyCompass is built to mirror that.

What's the same almost everywhere

The World Small Animal Veterinary Association (WSAVA) publishes the global guidelines that practices worldwide work from. Its Vaccination Guidelines define the international core vs. lifestyle split, and its Global Nutrition Guidelines standardise weight and body-condition assessment — the basis for a kcal target from logged weight. WSAVA's dental and pain-management guidelines are used internationally, and both WSAVA and the American Animal Hospital Association (AAHA) promote consistent, dated clinical histories. That shared backbone is why a well-kept owner record means the same thing in any consulting room.

And what differs — the bodies that set the standard of excellence in each region, and where the rules genuinely diverge:

Region Standard-setting bodies & teaching institutions What's standardised (global backbone) What's nationally specific
United States American Veterinary Medical Association (AVMA), AAHA, AAVMC; teaching: UC Davis, Cornell, Texas A&M WSAVA core/lifestyle model; body-condition & nutritional assessment; dated clinical records AAHA canine & feline vaccination guidelines; rabies vaccination legally mandated (state law); AAHA practice accreditation
United Kingdom Royal College of Veterinary Surgeons (RCVS), British Veterinary Association (BVA), BSAVA; teaching: Royal Veterinary College, Bristol, Edinburgh (Dick Vet) Same WSAVA vaccination & nutrition backbone; body-condition scoring Rabies-free status — no routine rabies vaccine; the GOV.UK pet travel scheme; RCVS Code of Professional Conduct
Ireland Veterinary Council of Ireland (VCI), Veterinary Ireland; teaching: UCD School of Veterinary Medicine WSAVA global guidelines; EU-harmonised welfare and record standards EU Pet Passport; rabies-free island regime aligned with the UK; national microchip database rules
Germany & the EU Bundestierärztekammer (German Veterinary Association), FECAVA; teaching: FU Berlin, Vetmeduni Vienna WSAVA/FECAVA vaccination & nutrition guidelines; body-condition scoring EU Pet Passport & EU Animal Health Law; mandatory rabies vaccination for travel; national StIKo Vet recommendations
Mexico & Latin America SENASICA (Mexico), WSAVA regional associations, FIAVAC; teaching: UNAM FMVZ, USP (Brazil) WSAVA core/lifestyle model & nutrition guidelines followed regionally Rabies endemic in parts of the region — rabies vaccination emphasised as core; national campaigns (e.g. Mexico's Semana Nacional de Vacunación Antirrábica)

FamilyCompass's UK / WSAVA / US region toggle is the practical expression of this: one global standard, with the regional differences the record actually needs. FamilyCompass is not affiliated with or endorsed by these organisations; the links are provided so you can read the standards for yourself.
