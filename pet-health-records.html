Global standards, local rules

Good pet record-keeping isn't invented country by country. Most of it is standardised worldwide — then a handful of national rules sit on top. FamilyCompass is built to mirror exactly that: one global backbone, with a region toggle for the parts that genuinely differ.

What's the same almost everywhere

The World Small Animal Veterinary Association (WSAVA) publishes the global guidelines that vets from London to Berlin to Dallas to Mexico City work from. Its Vaccination Guidelines define the worldwide split between core vaccines (recommended for every pet) and lifestyle vaccines (based on risk and where a pet lives). Its Global Nutrition Guidelines standardise how weight and body condition are assessed — the same thinking behind a kcal target calculated from logged weight — and its dental and pain-management guidelines are used internationally. That shared backbone is why a well-kept record travels: it means the same thing to a vet in any country.

Structured record-keeping is standardised too. The American Animal Hospital Association (AAHA) and WSAVA both promote consistent, dated clinical histories — the record FamilyCompass helps a pet owner keep between visits.

And what differs — a comparative view of the bodies that set the standard of excellence in each region, and where the rules genuinely diverge:

United Kingdom Royal College of Veterinary Surgeons (RCVS), British Veterinary Association (BVA), BSAVA; teaching: Royal Veterinary College, Bristol, Edinburgh (Dick Vet) Same WSAVA vaccination & nutrition backbone; body-condition scoring Rabies-free status — no routine rabies vaccine; the GOV.UK pet travel scheme; welfare guidance via PDSA
